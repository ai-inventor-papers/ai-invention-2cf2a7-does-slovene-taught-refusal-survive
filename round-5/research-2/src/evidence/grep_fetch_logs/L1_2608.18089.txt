Abstract
Instruction-tuned models often refuse harmful requests in English but comply with the same requests in
Yoruba, Igbo, Igala, and Hausa. This suggests that the refusal mechanism is present in the residual stream
but fails to activate for low-resource inputs. Recovering it normally requires labelled target-language

Latent Space Refusal Anchoring (LSR-Anchoring), a training-free method that extracts the

Introduction
Instruction-tuned models refuse harmful requests by activating a learned safety representation in the residual
stream [1, 14]. This mechanism is trained almost exclusively on English data. When harmful content arrives
in a low-resource language, the safety representation does not activate and the model complies [4, 6, 13].
Figure 1 shows this failure and its recovery under LSR-Anchoring.
