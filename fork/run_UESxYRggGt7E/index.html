Over-Refusal of Benign Slovene Prompts Survives English De-censoring in Gemma-3-12B

Gemma-3-12B-IT refuses harmless Slovene requests at nearly twice its English rate (false-alarm rate 0.569 vs. 0.364). Signal-detection analysis shows this is a criterion shift, not a sensitivity gap: the model sets a stricter threshold for compliance in Slovene without becoming any better at detecting actual harm. A controlled abliteration study, partially removing the English refusal direction, does not close this gap. The criterion-shift difference-in-differences is +0.52 [0.38, 0.68], replicated across four experiments.

Contributions

Over-refusal as criterion shift

Gemma false-refuses benign Slovene items at nearly twice the English rate. Signal-detection analysis attributes this to a criterion shift (DiDc = +0.52 [0.38, 0.68]), not a sensitivity difference (DiDd' = -0.003 [-0.33, 0.29]). The finding replicates across four independent experiments.

Reply-language refusal estimate

A crossed 3x3 design (prompt language x reply language) yields a raw output-language effect for Gemma (OUTSL = +1.14 [0.60, 1.56] log-odds), but prediction-powered inference correction and robustness checks weaken the finding to an estimate. The PPI-corrected contrast is 0.20 [-0.76, 1.13], consistent with zero.

Measurement methodology

Judge specificity, language compliance, and correction methods interact when evaluating multilingual refusal. The primary judge achieves only kappa = 0.12 agreement with the adjudicator on Slovene edited text. Multilingual safety evaluations that report only attack-success rates on harmful items will miss criterion shifts that inflate refusal rates on benign items.

Method

Models

Two 12-billion-parameter models are compared: Gemma-3-12B-IT (Google's standard multilingual post-training) and GaMS3-12B-Instruct (Slovene continued pretraining on 140 billion tokens plus supervised fine-tuning that is roughly 79% Slovene). Both share the same Gemma-3 pretrained ancestor and are loaded in NF4 quantization.

Abliteration edit

The abliteration edit applies per-layer directional ablation via LoRA adapters, optimized by a 40-trial TPE search. Each trial computes a refusal direction from contrastive pairs of harmful and benign English prompts and projects it out of the residual stream. Two edit doses are used throughout: a low dose (lambda = 0.5) and a high dose (lambda = 1.0 for Gemma, 0.625 for GaMS3), chosen to bracket the 50% English refusal point.

Over-refusal evaluation (criterion shift)

1,093 safe items (XSTest and OR-Bench) and 349 harmful items are tested in English and Slovene (machine-translated via NLLB). A Qwen3-14B judge classifies each response as refused or complied. Hit rate (refusal on harmful items) and false-alarm rate (refusal on safe items) yield signal-detection parameters d' (sensitivity) and c (criterion). The key contrast is the difference-in-differences on the criterion: DiDc = (cGaMS3,SL - cGaMS3,EN) - (cGemma,SL - cGemma,EN).

Crossed grid (reply-language refusal)

Three prompt languages (English, Slovene, Hungarian) are crossed with three reply languages in a 3x3 grid. Each cell contains 200 harmful items at three dose levels (zero, low, high). A suffix in the input language states the required response language, present in every cell. Cells where fewer than 90% of replies match the requested language are flagged. The output-language contrast OUTSL averages (X-to-SL minus X-to-EN) over prompt languages; the input-language contrast INSL is defined symmetrically.

Judge corrections

Judge accuracy is assessed against a Sonnet 4.5 adjudicator on 30 items per cell. PPI++ correction is reported alongside raw estimates. Experiment 16 uses a held-out 294-item set with a PPI estimator as the primary readout.

Results

Criterion shift on benign items

Gemma applies a stricter refusal criterion in Slovene than in English when processing benign items. GaMS3 does not. The entire language-dependent difference sits in the threshold, not in the model's ability to discriminate harmful from benign content.

The criterion shift replicates in Experiment 14 (high dose, Slovene-output cells: DiDc = -1.56 [-1.97, -1.29], Gemma minus GaMS3), in Experiment 16, and in an independent rescoring of Experiment 5 (DiDc in [0.39, 0.67]).

Reply-language refusal (estimate)

In the crossed 3x3 grid, edited Gemma at high dose refuses 0.56 to 0.83 when asked to reply in Slovene, but only 0.07 to 0.18 when asked to reply in English, regardless of prompt language. GaMS3 stays at 0.12 to 0.28 in every cell.

Figure 2. Refusal rates in the crossed prompt-language x reply-language design (200 harmful items per cell). (a) Gemma at high dose (lambda = 1.0): Slovene-output column refuses 0.56-0.83, English-output column 0.07-0.18. (b) GaMS3 at high dose (lambda = 0.625): rates stay at 0.12-0.28 throughout. Hatched cells have below 90% language compliance.

The raw output-language contrast for Gemma is OUTSL = +1.14 [0.60, 1.56] log-odds. The input-language contrast is INSL = -0.60 [-1.02, -0.18]: Slovene input reduces refusal, opposite to the output effect. The cross-model contrast is dOUTSL = -1.58 [-2.22, -0.66]: Gemma's output-language effect is larger than GaMS3's.

This finding is rated estimate for three reasons. First, the PPI-corrected output-language contrast is 0.20 [-0.76, 1.13], consistent with zero. Second, the four-readout robustness gate yields robust = False. Third, the harmful-content adjudicator agrees with the primary judge at only kappa = 0.12 in the Slovene cell. On compliant rows only (English-input), the edit-induced lag in the EN-to-SL cell is -1.26 [-2.17, 0.20], which includes zero.

Dose-response curves

Gemma's Slovene refusal curve is right-shifted relative to its English curve across the full dose range: at matched doses, more Slovene refusal remains. GaMS3 shows a smaller, non-significant shift that closes by lambda = 1.

Figure 3. Refusal rate vs. abliteration dose lambda (Experiment 9, 300 items at lambda = 0, 100 per edited step). Gemma's Slovene curve lies above its English curve at every dose (gap 0.21-0.32 between lambda 0.5 and 1). GaMS3's gap is smaller (at most 0.14) and closes by lambda = 1. The cross-model difference at matched 50% English refusal is G3 = -0.97 [-1.56, -0.44] log-odds (verdict: estimate; permutation p = 0.084).

Refusal composition

Of Gemma's Slovene refusals on benign items, 54% are explicit (the model states that it cannot help) and 26% are deflections (the model redirects the conversation). The benign false-refusal rate is 0.20 [0.10, 0.37] for Gemma EN-to-SL versus 0.10 [0.03, 0.26] for EN-to-EN.

Inconclusive and null results

The unedited GaMS3-vs-Gemma gap in the SL-to-SL cell is -1.17 [-2.83, 0.22], inconclusive: it includes zero and has a fragility index of 1. All Hungarian contrasts are uninformative (MDE approximately 3 log-odds). The RefusEU FINAL benchmark DiD is inconclusive (MDE > 2).

Limitations

  • Adjudication sample size. The adjudication sample is 30 items per cell, which limits the precision of judge-validity estimates. The false-alarm rate estimates rest on 1,093 safe items, adequate for the pooled contrast but noisy at the per-category level.
  • Judge specificity. In the Experiment 16 harmful-content adjudication, the primary judge achieves only kappa = 0.12 agreement with the adjudicator in the Gemma Slovene-edited cell. This inflates the raw refusal rate and weakens the reply-language finding. The PPI correction depends on the gold-label quality of the Sonnet 4.5 adjudicator, which is itself an LLM.
  • Language compliance. Four cells (SL-to-EN, HU-to-EN, SL-to-HU, HU-to-SL) fall below 90% language compliance, limiting the crossed-grid analysis to compliant rows for mechanism claims.
  • Quantization. Both models run in NF4 quantization. The interaction between quantization and directional ablation is not characterized.
  • Novelty. The observation that over-refusal rates differ across languages is partially anticipated by prior work. The contribution is the signal-detection decomposition and the controlled abliteration design, not the observation itself.
  • No human adjudication. The readout gate has never passed. All adjudication throughout the study is author-LLM, not human.
