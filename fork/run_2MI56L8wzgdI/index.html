Over-Refusal of Benign Slovene Prompts Survives English De-censoring in Gemma-3-12B

Gemma-3-12B-IT false-refuses benign Slovene prompts at a rate of 0.569, compared to 0.364 in English. A signal-detection analysis finds this gap is entirely a criterion shift (DiDc = +0.52 [0.38, 0.68]), not a sensitivity difference, replicated across four independent experiments. An English-only de-censoring edit does not close it. A secondary crossed-design experiment yields a raw output-language effect, but prediction-powered inference and weak judge anchoring reduce it to an estimate. The results indicate that multilingual safety evaluations should measure false-alarm rates on benign items, not only attack success on harmful ones.

Contributions

Primary finding

Over-refusal as criterion shift

Gemma false-refuses benign Slovene items at nearly twice the English rate. Signal-detection analysis attributes this to a criterion shift (DiDc = +0.52 [0.38, 0.68]) with no corresponding change in sensitivity (DiDd' = -0.003 [-0.33, 0.29]), replicated across four experiments.

Secondary analysis

Reply-language refusal (estimate)

A crossed 3 x 3 design yields a raw output-language effect for Gemma (OUTSL = +1.14 [0.60, 1.56] log-odds), but prediction-powered inference correction is consistent with zero (0.20 [-0.76, 1.13]) and the adjudicator agrees with the primary judge at only kappa = 0.12 in Slovene.

Methodology

Measurement methodology for multilingual refusal

The study quantifies how judge specificity, language compliance, and correction methods interact when evaluating multilingual refusal, showing that low specificity on edited Slovene text inflates raw refusal estimates.

Method

The study compares two 12-billion-parameter models built on the same base: Gemma-3-12B-IT and GaMS3-12B-Instruct, a Slovene-adapted variant with continued pretraining and DPO alignment. Both run in NF4 quantization.

Abliteration edit

The de-censoring intervention uses directional ablation of the refusal direction in the residual stream, applied via per-layer LoRA adapters optimized by a 40-trial tree-structured Parzen estimation (TPE) search. Each trial selects a layer subset, computes a refusal direction from contrastive pairs of harmful and benign English prompts, and projects it out. Two edit doses bracket the 50% English refusal point: a low dose (lambda = 0.5 for both models) and a high dose (lambda = 1.0 for Gemma, 0.625 for GaMS3).

Over-refusal measurement (signal detection)

Experiment 5 tests 1,093 safe items (XSTest + OR-Bench) and 349 harmful items in English and Slovene. A Qwen3-14B judge classifies each response as refused or complied. Hit rate (refusal on harmful) and false-alarm rate (refusal on safe) yield signal-detection parameters d' (sensitivity) and c (criterion). The key contrast: DiDc = (GaMS3SL - GaMS3EN) - (GemmaSL - GemmaEN).

Crossed grid (reply-language refusal)

Experiment 14 crosses three prompt languages (English, Slovene, Hungarian) with three reply languages in a 3 x 3 grid. Each cell contains 200 harmful items at three dose levels (zero, low, high). The output-language contrast OUTSL measures the mean log-odds refusal gap between Slovene-output and English-output cells, averaged over prompt languages. Cells where fewer than 90% of model replies match the requested language are flagged.

Judges and corrections

Judge accuracy is assessed against a Sonnet 4.5 adjudicator on 30 items per cell. Prediction-powered inference (PPI++) corrects the raw estimates against this gold standard. Experiment 16 uses a held-out set of 294 items as a PPI confirmation.

Reply-language refusal (estimate)

In the 3 x 3 crossed grid (Experiment 14), edited Gemma at high dose shows higher residual refusal when replying in Slovene than in English, regardless of prompt language. The raw mechanism contrast is OUTSL = +1.14 [0.60, 1.56] log-odds for Gemma; for GaMS3 the output-language contrast is -0.44 [-0.98, 0.27], spanning zero.

This finding is rated ESTIMATE. The PPI-corrected output-language contrast for Gemma is 0.20 [-0.76, 1.13], consistent with zero. The pre-registered four-readout robustness gate yields robust = False. The adjudicator agrees with the primary judge at kappa = 0.12 in the Gemma Slovene-edited cell (Se = 0.927, Sp = 0.714, n = 48). Experiment 16 provides a confirmatory point estimate (OUTGemma = +2.43 [+0.89, +5.38] on a held-out 294-item set), but the judge-specificity concern persists.

Dose-response

Experiment 9 measures refusal rates across nine lambda values (300 items per condition). Gemma's Slovene dose-response curve is right-shifted relative to its English curve: at matched doses, more Slovene refusal remains. GaMS3 shows a smaller, non-significant shift. The cross-model difference at matched 50% English refusal is G3 = -0.97 [-1.56, -0.44] log-odds (verdict: ESTIMATE; model-label permutation p = 0.084). The 13-step dense dose ladder (Experiment 15) confirms the gap is present across the full dose range.

Cross-model specificity

The cross-model output-language specificity contrast is dOUTSL = -1.58 [-2.22, -0.66] (MDE 1.07): Gemma's output-language effect is larger than GaMS3's. The input-language specificity contrast is dINSL = -0.26 [-1.13, 0.57] (MDE 1.19): the two models do not measurably differ in input-language sensitivity.

Compliant-row contrast

Restricting to rows where both models achieve over 90% language compliance (English-input rows only), the edit-induced refusal lag in the EN-to-SL cell is -1.26 [-2.17, 0.20], which includes zero.

Inconclusive and null results

The unedited GaMS3 shows a refusal gap of -1.17 [-2.83, 0.22] relative to Gemma in the SL-to-SL cell: inconclusive, with a fragility index of 1 and near-ceiling refusal rates. All Hungarian contrasts are uninformative (MDE approximately 3 log-odds units). The RefusEU validation (Experiment 17) on 1,300 items per language is inconclusive (MDE over 2), consistent with near-ceiling refusal rates.

Limitations

  • Sample size. The adjudication sample is 30 items per cell, limiting the precision of judge-validity estimates. The false-alarm rate estimates rest on 1,093 safe items, adequate for the pooled contrast but noisy at the per-category level.
  • Judge specificity. The primary judge achieves Sp = 0.714 in the Gemma Slovene-edited cell, meaning roughly 29% of non-refusals are misclassified as refusals. This inflates the raw refusal rate and weakens the reply-language finding. PPI correction depends on the gold-label quality of the Sonnet 4.5 adjudicator, itself an LLM.
  • Language compliance. Four cells (SL-to-EN, HU-to-EN, SL-to-HU, HU-to-SL) fall below 90% compliance, limiting the crossed-grid analysis to compliant rows for mechanism claims.
  • Quantization. Both models run in NF4 quantization. The interaction between quantization and directional ablation is not characterized.
  • Novelty. Over-refusal rates differ across languages is partially anticipated by prior work. The contribution is the signal-detection decomposition (criterion vs. sensitivity) and the controlled abliteration design, not the observation itself.
