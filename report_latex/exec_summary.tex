\section*{Goal}

GaMS3-12B-Instruct and Gemma-3-12B-IT share a pretrained ancestor (gemma-3-12b-pt) but diverge in safety supervision language: GaMS3 received Slovene continued pretraining and supervised fine-tuning with ${\sim}7{,}000$ safety-refusal examples drawn from machine-translated Qwen-family responses, while Gemma-IT received Google's standard multilingual post-training. Five iterations of experiments (23 artifacts total) tested whether Slovene-taught refusal transfers to English, whether English-only abliteration (Heretic) removes Slovene refusal equally in both models, and what mechanism explains any residual Slovene refusal.

\textbf{Criterion shift on original models.} Gemma over-refuses benign Slovene prompts (false-alarm rate 0.569 vs 0.364 in English), while GaMS3 is language-invariant (0.273 in both). The DiD in discrimination $d'$ is near zero ($-0.003$); the DiD in criterion $c$ is $+0.52$ [0.38, 0.68]. This replicates across experiments~5, 9, 14, and~16 on original models. On edited models the criterion-shift difference disappears (DiD$_c = 0.14$ [$-0.78$, 1.12]).

\textbf{Output-language effect (C-OUT).} Experiment~16 replicated on a fresh 294-item body with prediction-powered inference correction: edited Gemma refuses 2.43 log-odds more when told to answer in Slovene than in English. GaMS3 shows no output-language effect (OUT$_\text{GaMS3} = +0.12$ [$-0.47$, $+0.72$]). The input-language effect is negative in Gemma ($-0.68$ [$-1.23$, $-0.36$]): Slovene input reduces refusal. The signal-detection decomposition places the effect in the criterion, not discrimination.

\textbf{The lag is baseline (R-BASE).} The cross-lingual abliteration lag is better characterised as a pre-edit model difference than an edit-induced effect. On experiment~15's fine-grained lambda ladder (MDE 0.42, the study's most powerful estimate), the edit-induced component is $+0.47$ [$-0.96$, 2.14] and both models' curves run parallel with slopes near 1. In every item body, the pre-edit baseline $G_{3,\text{orig}}$ is negative.

\textbf{Reserve is safety, not production failure.} Gemma's Slovene-output reserve produces less harmful content, not just more refusal: the harmful-content output-language contrast is 2.61 [2.20, 3.07] at high dose. The four-class decomposition shows half of the non-compliance is deflection (production-failure index difference 0.46 [0.23, 0.65]), consistent with the relevance curse.

\section*{What the Edit Does on RefusEU}

On the full 1,300-item RefusEU FINAL reserved split (experiment~17), the Heretic edit at $\lambda = 1.0$ is effective in all four model$\times$language cells:

The edit is more effective on GaMS3 than Gemma in both languages. The English and Slovene prompt sets are different (LaBSE similarity 0.55), so within-model SL-vs-EN differences are not a pure language effect.

\section*{Utility Control}

The English-only abliteration edit does not damage Slovene downstream skills more than English ones. All macro headroom-normalised losses are below 0.05. Language asymmetry $A = H_\text{SL} - H_\text{EN}$ is positive (Slovene hurt less) in every cell. Random-adjusted excess asymmetry excludes zero for GaMS3 ($+0.051$ [$+0.012$, $+0.093$]). Localised costs: GaMS3 English ARC-Challenge ($-3.7$\,pp, $p = 0.041$) and BoolQ ($-2.0$/$-2.3$\,pp). Belebele comprehension and FLORES fluency are unaffected.

\section*{Evidence Quality Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The readout gate has never passed.} Every LLM judge has kappa at most 0.43 on edited rows against blind adjudication; the paid gemini frontier judge has specificity 0.66--0.73 (over-calls REFUSE). All blind adjudication is author-LLM, not human.
  \item \textbf{The frozen-core Heretic procedure} (bf16, 80 trials, Heretic's selection rule) has never been run. Every edit is an iteration-1 reduced-trial NF4 fallback.
  \item \textbf{Natural RefusEU reserved-split prompts} have never been scored under the edit (experiment~17 used the PolyGuard readout, not the pre-registered gemini judge).
  \item \textbf{R-BASE baseline is fragile} in most item bodies: experiment~15's fragility index is 1 (one Gemma SL flip at $\lambda = 0$ moves the baseline inside the margin). Only experiment~9 has a robust offset (fragility index 9).
  \item \textbf{The harmful-content adjudicator kappa} is 0.12 in experiment~16 (gate requires 0.60), traced to truncation handling. Refusal kappa is 0.90.
  \item \textbf{GaMS3 failed the output-language manipulation} off the English-input row (SL$\to$EN compliance 0.00 in experiment~14), so the input-vs-output decomposition is unavailable for GaMS3.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Novelty}

The main claim is \textbf{PARTIALLY OCCUPIED} (downgraded from NOVEL). Two preprints partly occupy the lane: Addagada (2609.08373) forces the output language and finds a relevance curse; Nguyen et al.\ (2608.26186) cross prompt and response language on benign content. The narrowed design---crossing both factors with safety endpoints, a sibling pair sharing a pretrained ancestor, partial-dose edit plus a public checkpoint, Hungarian as a third language, and a constant suffix in all cells---remains unoccupied as of September 2026 (medium-low confidence).

\section*{Open Items for Future Work}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
  \item C-OUT deserves pre-registered confirmation with a validated readout and human adjudication on edited rows.
  \item If R-BASE is correct, the lag is an artefact of model selection (GaMS3 was trained to refuse less in Slovene), not a property of the abliteration procedure.
  \item The dose contrast (main claim vs alternate~1) remains underpowered across all five iterations; a larger item body or a different operationalisation of ``dose'' may be needed.
  \item The M-OUT production-fluency account (refusal as a fallback when the model must produce harmful content in a language it generates poorly) was hypothesised but never directly tested.
\end{enumerate}
